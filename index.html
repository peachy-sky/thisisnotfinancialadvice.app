wait, what even is this?

The money class your school forgot to teach — except it's a game and actually fun.

In This Is Not Financial Advice; The Money Learning Game, you play through 5–40 rounds representing your life — years of actual financial decisions with actual consequences. (Simulated ones. Don't panic.) Each round, you draw a hand of cards: accounts to open, life events to navigate, and choices that will ripple forward in ways that may delight or haunt you.

Open a checking account, stash money in a high-yield savings account, invest in a Roth IRA, or try your hand at a brokerage. Pay rent. Handle insurance. Deal with surprise expenses (the game's personal favorite trick). Watch your credit score react to every single decision you make — in real time.

At the end of the rounds, you see your net worth, your score, and a projection of where you'd land by retirement if you kept those habits up. It's a mirror — and a motivator.

meet the creator

Hi, I'm Chloe.

Heyyo! My name is Chloe, and I am the twentysomething sidequester behind This Is Not Financial Advice. I have always been passionate about financial freedom (ever since my first "business" in 4th grade!), and will talk your ear off over it for hours if you let me.

I believe that financial freedom is not about the money — it's not about the math, and it's not about the numbers. Ultimately, financial freedom is about the human life it impacts, the doors it opens, and the human being that it empowers.

Over the years, I had been shocked and a little horrified to learn just how few people were taught personal financial education in school. I try to never let a friend of mine enter the adult life without some good money basics, so it's a dream to finally be building something to help share those good money basics with younger students.

I want to inspire kids to get excited about personal finance — really excited — and strive for the lives they can lead when money isn't a worry but a point of pride.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is TINFA for?

Built for ages 11 and up, but honestly it's for anyone who never got a real personal finance class — plenty of adults play too, no judgment.

How long does a game take?

A round represents one year, and a full game runs 5–40 rounds depending on how you set it up — anywhere from a quick 20-minute playthrough to a full "live your whole financial life" session.

Do I need to know anything about money first?

Nope — that's the whole point. The game teaches the concepts as you play: savings, investing, taxes, credit, insurance, all of it.
